## Notes on the draft, and why it reads the way it does

- **IT MATCHES WHAT THE PDFs ACTUALLY SAY.** No live subscription, no GMP, no
  native mobile app, no "IPO catalogue management" — all of those were removed
  from the broker document deliberately. An email that promises more than the
  attachment delivers is the fastest way to lose a compliance reader.
- **The call to action is "reply to this email."** The PDFs no longer carry a
  URL, so the email is the only route — which is also the route that cannot go
  stale in a forwarded attachment.
- **It names the operating entity in the first line.** A cold email from an
  unknown sender to a regulated firm is read with suspicion; the legal name,
  stated immediately, is what makes it answerable.
- **It says which document goes to compliance.** A broker's first decision is
  whether to forward it, not whether to buy — so the email makes that forward
  easy.
- **The disclaimer is the PDF's own**, kept word for word rather than
  paraphrased, so the email and the attachment cannot say two different things.
- **No figures are quoted.** Catalogue size and coverage are on the cover of the
  attachment; repeating them in the body only creates two numbers to keep in
  step.

### Before the first send

- **Prove SMTP.** No email from this platform has ever been verified end to end
  (see CLAUDE.md). This is a hand-sent message from a normal mailbox, so it is
  unaffected — but do not assume the platform's own mail works because this did.
- If you want the PDFs to stand alone when forwarded without the email, tell me
  and I will put a contact address or number back on the closing panel. That was
  the deliberate choice to leave out.
