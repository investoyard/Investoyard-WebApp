How it works — the full picture, in plain words

One system, sold three ways

The complete design of our platform — explained without technical jargon, so anyone can follow every part.

1

The big idea

We build one single system and offer it in three business models. Between the three, only two things ever change:

  • 1.Who is allowed to see the data — a branch, a partner, or us.
  • 2.Whose brand shows on the screen — Investoyard's, or the partner's own.

Everything else — the IPO data, the apply process, the rules — is shared. That's why we never build three separate products.

2

The three models

  • •Direct (B2C): customers come to Investoyard, sign up and apply themselves. Our name, our website.
  • •Partner with branches: a partner company joins us and opens many branches. Each branch sees only its own customers; the partner's head office sees all its branches.
  • •White-label: a partner uses their own website and logo. Customers feel it's fully theirs — but it runs on our same system inside.
🏢 Picture one building we own. We build the structure, lifts and security once. Different businesses operate inside through their own doors — but it's the same building underneath.
3

Everyone is a "tenant" in a family tree

Every business using the system is a tenant — think of a renter in our building. We arrange them in a simple family tree: Investoyard at the top, partners below, and each partner's branches under it.

Our own direct customers sit in a channel called Investoyard Direct. So the rule stays simple: every customer belongs to a channel — Investoyard Direct, or a partner's branch. And each record carries a small label for which tenant it belongs to — that's what keeps everything separated.

Investoyard is the operator (sees all). Every customer lives in a channel — Investoyard Direct (B2C), or a partner's branch.
4

How the system knows "who is this for?"

On every page — even the public landing page where nobody is logged in — our server first reads the web address (domain) the page was opened on, and instantly loads the right brand:

  • →Opened on a partner's own domain (e.g. ipo.partnerbank.com)? → show that partner's logo, colours & name.
  • →Opened on our domain (investoyard.com)? → show our brand.
  • →Once a partner/branch staff member logs in, the login also decides which data they're allowed to see.
Decided approach: the brand is picked from the web address, on our server, the moment a page loads — no login needed. This is the same host-based logic Investoyard already uses today; we're formalising it into the partner list.
5

Keeping each other's data private

Every screen automatically shows a person only what they're allowed to see. Staff do nothing special — the system filters it, every single time:

A branchsees only its own customers
A partner's head officesees all its branches together
Investoyard (us)sees everything

Double lock. On top of the automatic filter, there's a second lock inside the database itself — so even a mistake in the software can never leak one partner's data to another.

6

Launching a white-label = filling a form

When a new partner wants their own branded version, we don't write new software. We just enter their details:

  • ✓Upload their logo and pick their colours
  • ✓Switch options on/off (e.g. hide certain unofficial data if their rules require)
  • ✓Register their website address → from then on, anyone opening that address automatically sees their brand

That's it — a setup that takes days, not months, because it's all settings, not new development.

8

What we store (kept simple)

  • •A "belongs-to" label on each customer and application (which tenant owns it).
  • •IPO information is shared by everyone — it's public market data, the same for all.
  • •When a customer signs up through a partner, we also record their permission to share data with that partner — required by India's data-protection law (DPDP).
  • •Applying to an IPO goes to the exchange (NSE/BSE) through licensed banker credentials. A partner can use ours, or plug in their own — the system supports both.
  • •A person's PAN is unique within a partner, but the same PAN can exist under another partner (they may be a customer in two places).
  • •One PAN can apply in up to three ways per IPO — as an Individual, a Shareholder, or an Employee (once each). The admin chooses which of these each IPO allows.

10

Safety & rules we never skip

  • ✓Data is walled off automatically, with a backup lock in the database.
  • ✓A partner's website address is verified as truly theirs before it goes live.
  • ✓Passwords & secret keys are kept in a locked vault.
  • ✓We keep an activity log for each partner.
  • ✓By law, every applicant always uses their own PAN, bank and UPI.
  • ✓We regularly test that one partner can never see another's data.
  • ✓An independent, government-approved (CERT-In) security company does the security testing (VAPT) — trying to break in both as an outsider with no access (black-box) and with full access to our code (white-box) — before launch and regularly after.
  • ✓No one can use our back-end (API) directly. Every request must carry a valid, signed login or it's rejected — and the back-end isn't even reachable from the open internet except through our guarded gateway.
  • ✓We follow the applicable SEBI cyber-security rules, CERT-In incident-reporting, and India's data-protection law (DPDP) — and data is kept in India.
11

What it's built with

All modern, widely-used, proven technologies — and one programming language (TypeScript) everywhere, so the same small team builds the website, mobile app and back-end efficiently.

  • •Website: React / Next.js — fast, SEO-friendly, and installable like an app.
  • •Mobile app: React Native — one codebase runs on Android & iOS.
  • •Back-end: Node.js (NestJS) with a PostgreSQL database. (SQL Server is also supported.)
  • •IPO submission: connects to NSE & BSE through the licensed banker rail.
  • •Runs on the cloud — secure and scalable, with sensitive data kept in a protected vault.
12

Handling heavy traffic

IPO sites get a huge rush in the last hour before a popular IPO closes. We keep the site fast in two simple ways — and it costs nothing extra:

  • 1.We cache the pages & data. Most visitors are just looking at IPO, GMP and subscription info — we serve them a ready-made copy from memory (refreshed every few seconds) instead of rebuilding it each time. Thousands of visitors, almost no extra work.
  • 2.We queue the "Apply" rush. Every application joins a line (queue) and is sent to the exchange (NSE/BSE) at a steady pace — so even 50,000 people applying at once can't overwhelm anything. Each person instantly sees "submitted".

And because we know the IPO calendar, we can add extra server power automatically on busy days, and slow down non-essential refreshes if things ever get extreme — so the site stays up and fast.
